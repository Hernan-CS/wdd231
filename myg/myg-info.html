About the Use of English Test

Welcome to Moyinglish, your companion to mastering the Use of English Test (Parts 1-4). This page gives you a clear overview of the test structure, what each part assesses, and how your proficiency maps to internationally recognized levels. Use it as your roadmap to plan and track your preparation.

Test Structure - 4 Core Parts

Part 1: Grammar & Vocabulary (Multiple Cloze Choice)

Focused on grammar accuracy and vocabulary knowledge, you'll choose the correct word or phrase among options. Great for testing your command of tenses, collocations, modal verbs, prepositions, and more.

Part 2: Open Cloze (Gap-Fill)

Tests reading comprehension and lexical resource: you fill in missing words within a short text. You need to rely on context and collocation knowledge.

Part 3: Word-formation

You'll convert words to the required form (e.g., noun to adjective) to fit a sentence. This checks your understanding of derivational morphology and collocations.

Part 4: Key Word Transformations

This part assesses your ability to rewrite sentences using a given key word, without changing meaning. It examines grammar, vocabulary, and paraphrasing skills, which is essential for advanced writing and speaking.

CEFR Levels & What They Mean for You

CEFR LevelDescription
A2Basic user - Can handle simple, routine tasks and understand basic texts.
B1Intermediate - Can deal with everyday situations and produce simple connected text.
B2Upper-intermediate - Able to understand main ideas of complex texts and communicate with some fluency and spontaneity.
C1Advanced - Can use English flexibly and effectively for academic, professional or social purposes.
C2Proficient - Near-native ability in comprehension and expression.

If you're preparing for the Use of English Test with the aim to reach B2 or above, focus especially on Parts 3 and 4, they often challenge learners transitioning from intermediate to advanced level.

Study & Preparation Tips

  • Practice each part separately to build confidence: grammar drills, cloze exercises, word-formation drills, and transformation tasks.
  • Read a variety of texts daily, such as news, short stories, articles, to build vocabulary and context awareness.
  • Review and analyse your mistakes. Understand why an answer is wrong before moving on.
  • Time yourself during practice sessions. Familiarity with the test format and timing reduces stress on exam day.
  • Use the Exercises section to simulate real test conditions, then review with detailed feedback.
